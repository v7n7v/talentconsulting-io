# Career-suite competitors: Teal, Huntr, Jobscan

Checked 2026-10-08 against official product, pricing/help, and release pages. Vendor product descriptions and pricing below are vendor claims; user/customer outcome claims are not independently verified here. I found no independent side-by-side test of current releases in this research. Huntr publishes its own hands-on comparisons, which are disclosed as Huntr-created. No revenue, customer-count, or conversion conclusions are inferred.

## Teal

**Audience and job to be done.** Broad job seekers who need a repeatable workspace: build multiple resumes, tailor each to a posting, collect jobs from elsewhere, and track applications. The free tier serves as the low-friction entry point; Teal+ sells higher limits and more detailed AI/design tools.

**What it advertises.** Unlimited free resume versions and job tracking, resume imports (including LinkedIn), browser extension, ATS-style templates, job description keyword/match analysis, AI summaries/bullets/cover letters, and application organization. Teal+ lists unlimited advanced analysis/matching and AI, premium templates/design controls, and more. Its public pricing page currently lists $13 per 7 days, $29 per 30 days, and $79 per 90 days. Terms say subscriptions renew automatically until cancelled; refund requests have plan-dependent windows (2 days weekly, 7 monthly, 14 quarterly/annual) and are reviewed case by case. The pricing page says cancel any time, access continues through the paid period.

**Distribution/onboarding advantage visible in product.** The free builder, unlimited free tracker, resume/LinkedIn import, and job-saving extension create several entry points before upgrade. It advertises browser-only use and no setup; its tracker can acquire postings from other sites. Those are observable product choices, not proof of acquisition cost or conversion.

**Recent AI direction.** Teal’s Help Center documents an Offer Analysis tool that parses an offer letter and offers follow-up questions to a “Compensation AI Agent.” Access requires at least five bookmarked jobs. This is a bounded post-offer decision aid, distinct from the resume-tailoring workflow.

**Taco should match / can avoid.** Match reliable import, job-specific resume tailoring, editable output, document export, and low-friction try-before-pay. Taco’s source-fact guardrails and reviewable changes are a credible distinction to make explicit. It can avoid replicating a full job CRM, extension, broad template catalog, and compensation analysis until a first buyer asks; those widen scope without validating Taco’s differentiator.

## Huntr

**Audience and job to be done.** Active applicants seeking one place for resumes, job tracking, contact/application data, and repeated applications. Its positioning includes both tailoring and application autofill, particularly useful for people managing multiple opportunities.

**What it advertises.** Free base resumes and PDF export; all templates; basic matching/scoring; limited AI review/tailor; up to 100 tracked jobs; Chrome job clipper, map view, contact management, and application autofill. Pro adds unlimited AI resume generations and tailored resumes, cover letters, advanced matching/scoring/insights, unlimited tracking, and unlimited document uploads. Huntr’s help article dated May 1, 2026 lists Pro at $40 monthly, $90 quarterly, or $160 every six months; cancel anytime, refunds based on usage/support contact. Its public product page also advertises free PDF export and application autofills.

**Distribution/onboarding advantage visible in product.** Users can start with a no-cost base resume and PDF, save a job from the browser, then track it and autofill applications. The combination gives the user reasons to keep Huntr open across the application cycle. Huntr publicly says 500,000+ users have used it; that is a vendor-reported figure, not independently substantiated here.

**Recent AI direction.** Official help documents AI Review for feedback and AI Tailor for applying job-posting language/skills to a resume, with suggested changes the user can apply, edit, or customize. Its May 2026 plan page describes these capabilities, but I found no current official evidence of a Huntr autonomous or multi-agent runtime. Huntr’s February/April 2026 product comparison posts report hands-on tests, but are written by Huntr and rank Huntr first; they should be treated as self-published evaluation, not independent evidence.

**Taco should match / can avoid.** Match the inspectable tailoring loop, feedback, export, and enough job context to personalize accurately. Taco’s provenance-backed factual edits can be stronger if every proposed claim is tied to source facts. Avoid competing on a general-purpose tracker, contact CRM, map, and autofill until customer discovery establishes that these solve the first customer’s pain.

Sources: [Huntr plans/pricing (May 2026)](https://help.huntr.co/en/articles/10714568-plan-types-and-pricing) · [AI Review and AI Tailor walkthrough](https://help.huntr.co/en/articles/11535512-ai-review-ai-tailor-walkthrough) · [Huntr product overview](https://huntr.co/)

## Jobscan

**Audience and job to be done.** Applicants optimizing for ATS matching and wanting a wider path from resume building through job discovery and submission. The brand’s core promise is to improve match quality; recent launches extend that into guided resume creation and application preparation.

**What it advertises.** Resume/job-description Match Reports, ATS-specific optimization, LinkedIn optimization, resume builder/manager, cover letters, tracker, and now AI Resume Coach and Auto Apply. The Coach asks one question at a time, turns responses into resume content, supports accept/edit/skip, and carries the resume to the optimizer. Auto Apply matches roles from Greenhouse, Lever, Workable, and 20+ ATS platforms; drafts application answers from the user’s resume/preferences; shows editable fields and requires user approval before submission. Premium users receive two Auto Apply credits, topped up monthly; extra credits cost $8.50/5, $15/10, or $35/25. Jobscan’s official 2026 [comparison page](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/jobscan-vs-teal/) lists Premium at $49.95 monthly or $89.95 quarterly; a 7-day trial is offered on the quarterly plan per its promotions/cancellation pages. Premium renews on the selected monthly/quarterly cycle until cancelled; cancelling ends access at period end, and refunds are limited and usage-dependent. Confirm the checkout price before purchase because offers can change.

**Distribution/onboarding advantage visible in product.** A free resume scan and free resume builder/download give users a direct first task. Resume upload or LinkedIn import and its ATS-focused content funnel bring users into a specific job-to-resume comparison. Auto Apply adds a preference form (the company says about two minutes), resume parsing, matched job feed, and centralized review queue. Jobscan reports Auto Apply as part of its integrated product; this is a product claim, not independently measured throughput or placement performance.

**Recent AI direction.** Official release articles announce Auto Apply on June 10, 2026 and the AI Resume Builder/Coach on July 8, 2026. These are supervised, multi-step workflows: agent-like job matching and form drafting with a human approval checkpoint, plus interview-style resume intake. They represent the strongest current adjacent automation of the three competitors researched.

**Taco should match / can avoid.** Match ATS-readable documents, job-specific comparisons, guided intake, and clear user approval before changes or submissions. Taco can distinguish itself with explicit source-fact provenance and change review as a first-class promise. It can avoid building job discovery, LinkedIn optimization, auto-apply, or a complete career suite before proving that its resume transformation produces trusted, useful applications.

Sources: [Jobscan Auto Apply](https://www.jobscan.co/auto-apply) · [AI Resume Builder / Coach](https://www.jobscan.co/ai-resume) · [2026 release: Auto Apply](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/auto-apply/) · [Premium pricing](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/jobscan-vs-teal/)

## Implication for Taco’s first customer

These competitors show that resume tailoring is now a baseline feature inside free-to-start suites, and Jobscan is extending into supervised application execution. Taco’s best near-term wedge is narrower and verifiable: take a person’s existing resume and a target posting, produce an editable tailored version, and show exactly which source facts support each change. Sell a concrete pilot around reviewed applications and time-to-approved-resume, not promised interviews or placements. Keep the existing PDF/Word and template workflow; instrument review acceptance, edits/reverts, and completion time so Taco can substantiate outcomes before making them a marketing claim. No first-customer outcome has yet been substantiated.
