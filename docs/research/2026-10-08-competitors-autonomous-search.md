# Competitive scan: autonomous job search services

**Checked 8 October 2026.** Vendor pages describe their own offers; application and interview outcome figures are marketing claims, not independently verified results. “Active” below means a current public product surface was visible on this date, not that I tested a paid account or completed an application.

## AIApply

**Offer and outcome.** AIApply’s Auto Apply sells application volume: **$49/month for 100 applications, $99/month for 250, or $199/quarter for 750**. Job-specific resume and cover-letter tailoring are separate **$12/month each** add-ons. A credit equals a submitted application. Its help article claims users see “up to 80% higher interview rates” versus manual applications; that is a vendor claim, not an independently established outcome. ([plans and feature details](https://support.aiapply.co/en/articles/14216708-how-does-auto-apply-works))

**Automation and activation.** Users choose Auto, Hybrid, or Review mode. Auto submits matching applications without approval; Hybrid auto-submits matches at 75%+ and holds lower scores; Review requires approval for every job. After purchase and profile setup, the current instructions say activity can begin the same day. AIApply also offers a web and iPhone app with a shared application dashboard and inbox, so it can engage candidates on mobile as well as during desktop setup. ([Auto Apply modes and setup](https://support.aiapply.co/en/articles/15692829-how-auto-apply-works), [iPhone app guide](https://support.aiapply.co/en/articles/16764354-getting-started-with-the-aiapply-app))

**Advantage Taco would face.** The product makes the promise immediately legible (“apply for me”), puts paid application quotas beside it, and reduces time-to-first-action with ready-to-run automation and mobile access. Taco should not promise comparable volume or interview lift.

## Sonara

**Status and offer.** Sonara’s current site is live, presents current job listings, and identifies **Bold Limited** as copyright owner. Sonara’s official account announced joining the BOLD family; the current product is an acquired brand under BOLD, not simply the original startup assumed to have continued unchanged. Current public product pages reviewed did not expose a specific price. Its terms say recurring billing and any trial terms are shown on the subscription/offer page; confirm the amount and renewal interval at checkout rather than repeating historical prices. ([Sonara’s official BOLD announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sonaraapply_futureofwork-careertech-jobsearchinnovation-activity-7331429246852042752-ZgXm), [current product site](https://www.sonara.ai/), [terms and billing rules](https://www.sonara.ai/terms-conditions))

**Automation and activation.** The current product pitch is daily matching plus application completion: choose jobs from its curated queue and Sonara can fill forms. Its terms say AI matches against profile/preferences, then applies at the user’s request; the user can modify or delete queued jobs. This documents a review/control step before the request to apply, rather than a clearly documented global “submit everything” default. Role/location landing pages and current listings create search-engine entry points; ownership by BOLD, an established career-services portfolio, is a plausible distribution advantage, though the reviewed pages do not quantify cross-brand traffic. Sonara’s “10x as many applications” wording is its own claim, not a measured Taco benchmark.

## LoopCV

**Offer and outcome.** LoopCV’s official pricing page offers a **free plan with no card** and says paid plans start at **€9.99/month**, buying higher volume, priority processing, and advanced filters. Its help material says plans vary by application method and that exact support/limits depend on the loop configuration. The site also advertises three-times-more-interviews and “61% interview in 10 days”; those are vendor-stated outcome claims, not independent evidence. ([pricing and free-tier details](https://www.loopcv.pro/pricing/), [homepage claims and listed customer segments](https://www.loopcv.pro/), [official automation knowledge base](https://loopcv.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/103000399849-knowledge-base))

**Automation and activation.** Users configure job titles and locations; email and supported form applications can run automatically. Some authenticated “Easy Apply” flows require the Chrome extension and user confirmation/run, and applications needing unsupported logins remain manual. Higher plans enable AI answers from a CV/profile and prior answers; the answer can be viewed and edited. The free automated tier is a powerful acquisition loop: sign up, upload a CV, set preferences, get real application activity without a payment step. Extension support spans LinkedIn, Indeed, Dice, Reed, StepStone, and GulfTalent. The site also explicitly serves career coaches, universities, bootcamps, and outplacement providers, suggesting partner distribution paths in addition to job-seeker SEO. The help page documents these product boundaries; I did not independently test coverage. ([automation settings and application methods](https://loopcv.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/103000399849-knowledge-base))

## What Taco should do to get its first paying customer

Auto-apply volume is a poor first battleground: LoopCV gives a real version away free, and AIApply sells hundreds of submissions at known prices. Taco’s current credible value is a guided, source-supported transformation of a resume for a real target job, reviewed before export. Its scout/packet workflow and bounded tool chat support a focused result; the durable mission architecture is still proposed, not built, and must not be sold as shipped capability.

Test a **one-job paid application packet** for an active seeker: use the current guided workflow to tailor the resume to one posting, expose the supporting facts and edits for approval, then deliver the existing exports. A **one-time $19–$29 pilot price** is a hypothesis to test, not an established market-clearing price. Promise a reviewable, finished application artifact and time saved; do not promise interviews or a job. Seek the first buyer through a setting with a live target role (for example, a career coach or small cohort) and learn whether they will pay for accuracy and confidence, not just more submissions. Track checkout, completion, accepted/edited/rejected changes, and time-to-approved export. Only add autonomous execution if paying users identify it as the obstacle after this outcome is delivered.
