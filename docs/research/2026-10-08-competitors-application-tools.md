# Competitor scan: application and job-search tools

**Checked 8 October 2026.** This is a scan of vendors’ own public product, help, and pricing pages. Feature, audience, and outcome claims below are vendor claims, not independently validated results.

## Rezi

Rezi sells an ATS-focused resume product to individual job seekers, with a separate institutional offer for universities, agencies, and career services. The free plan lets a user create one resume, try limited scoring/keyword analysis and AI features, and download three PDFs. Its pricing page lists Pro at **$29/month**, Lifetime at **$149 one-time**, and Enterprise at **$99/month per 200 users**; Pro includes unlimited resumes, AI tools, exports, templates, and a monthly human review. It advertises a 30-day refund guarantee. ([pricing](https://www.rezi.ai/pricing))

The strongest workflow story is resume-first but conversational: users can start from scratch, import a PDF/DOCX, bring in LinkedIn details through a Chrome extension, or build/edit a resume with the AI Resume Agent. The agent can analyze and rewrite resume sections, tailor against a job description, suggest bullets, help prepare for interviews, and recommend jobs. Rezi describes follow-up conversation and resume-attached feedback; its guide presents recommendations for users to review and apply. ([create-resume guide](https://www.rezi.ai/rezi-docs/create-resume), [AI Resume Agent guide](https://www.rezi.ai/rezi-docs/ai-resume-agent))

**What the buyer gets:** quick resume production and ATS-oriented checks, with enough free utility to try before paying; Pro buys unlimited use and expert review. Distribution is self-serve web, a no-card free entry, LinkedIn import via extension, and an enterprise/career-service channel.

**Implication for Taco:** “AI resume builder,” score, keyword match, rewrite, and chat are already table stakes. Taco’s sharper distinction is a guided job-specific transformation that shows what changed and keeps source facts locked for review, paired with saved/exportable Word/PDF and templates. Avoid framing this as a superior ATS score or guaranteed interview result.

## Simplify

Simplify positions itself as an AI job-search agent for candidates, connecting profile, job discovery, applications, documents, networking, and tracking. Its free product centers on job matches/search, basic builder, tracker, and Copilot. Simplify+ adds job-specific resume tailoring, cover letters, application-question responses, outreach, networking, and job analytics; the published terms are **$19.99 for one week, $39.99 for one month, or $89.99 for three months**, auto-renewing unless canceled. ([Simplify+ features and pricing](https://help.simplify.jobs/en/help/articles/5623502-whats-included-in-simplify-features-and-pricing), [signup and onboarding](https://help.simplify.jobs/articles/0738509-signing-up-for-simplify))

Onboarding asks for role/location/pay preferences and resume upload, parses the resume into a reusable profile, then presents profile-strength tasks and job matches. The browser extension is its distribution wedge: Copilot fills supported application portals, creates job-specific writing in the flow, and tracks applications. Saved application information is reused through the profile; Simplify says repeated answers can be reused and generated responses remain editable. ([signup and onboarding](https://help.simplify.jobs/articles/0738509-signing-up-for-simplify), [Simplify+ features and pricing](https://help.simplify.jobs/en/help/articles/5623502-whats-included-in-simplify-features-and-pricing))

The Autopilot agent is a consequential differentiator, but its current promise has a boundary: beta access is limited to some Simplify+ members; when selected, it tailors, fills, and submits without a review step. It fails rather than guesses when uncertain; pausing to ask questions and scheduled auto-apply are described as future work in the help article. ([Autopilot beta guide](https://help.simplify.jobs/help/articles/1784339-getting-started-with-autopilot))

**What the buyer gets:** less repetitive work across a high-volume search, with a persistent profile, matching, autofill, and tracking in one system. Taco should not compete on breadth of job inventory or portal coverage. Sell the more careful “make this application accurate and compelling” moment before the candidate submits it; keep human approval central.

## Careerflow

Careerflow’s audience is job seekers managing a broad search, with tools spanning resume and LinkedIn optimization, job/networking trackers, recruiter search, interview practice, cover letters, and autofill. Its workflow starts with a profile/resume, then uses a browser extension to save jobs, tailor materials, fill supported forms, and track progress. The autofill product page says the candidate reviews and edits fields and submits; onboarding asks the user to upload a resume and fill in work history, education, and contact details. ([autofill product page](https://www.careerflow.ai/job-autofill), [resume builder](https://www.careerflow.ai/resume-builder))

Pricing is not a clean single benchmark: the official plan page shows **Free**, and visible Premium cards at **$8.99/week** or **$23.99/month**; Premium Plus is shown at **$19.99/week** or **$44.99/month**. The same page also renders annual discounts and multiple totals, so confirm the checkout term before comparing prices. Free includes limited resume builder, autofill, tracker, networking, and document storage; Premium advertises unlimited AI resumes, ATS optimization, cover letters, LinkedIn tools, and support; Premium Plus adds mock interviews and interview analysis. ([plans and pricing](https://www.careerflow.ai/premium))

**What the buyer gets:** a low-friction free entry and a cross-step toolkit, with a browser extension that follows the job seeker across sites. Its advertised “career copilot” is a suite of specialized functions; the cited pages document reusable profile data and review-before-submit autofill. Do not infer durable conversational memory or claim the entire autofill coverage promise is proven.

## Position for first revenue

Taco should sell a **reviewable, job-specific application sprint** to an active candidate with a real target role: upload and diagnose an existing resume, map it to one job description, make and review fact-locked edits, and leave with a polished template plus PDF/Word files. This is easier to explain and charge for than “another all-in-one AI career copilot.” The practical proof is the candidate’s before/after application and their approval of each material change, not an ATS score or employment guarantee.

The existing Taco workflow supports upload/diagnose → job description → AI morph/review with fact locks → templates, PDF/Word, and save, plus suite tools and bounded chat/scout. Position those as a focused workflow, not as demonstrated hiring outcomes. Durable agent architecture remains a proposal; it should not appear in sales claims until built and validated. First customer learning should test whether a candidate will pay for confidence, accuracy, and a finished application for a consequential role—not whether Taco can match competitors’ feature count.
