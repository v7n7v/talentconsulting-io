# Taco SEO market research and implementation plan

**Research date:** 9 October 2026  
**Audience assumption:** US English-speaking job seekers. This is provisional; confirm with Search Console and customer data before expanding to other markets.  
**Scope:** Search intent, first-party competitor positioning, public-page architecture and a staged SEO plan. This is qualitative market research, not a keyword-volume forecast or ranking report.

## Executive assessment

Taco can compete for relevant job-seeker searches by making its complete workflow easy to understand: bring a real resume, compare it with role requirements, see evidence and gaps, tailor with controlled changes, choose a template, then review and export. The public home page already communicates role fit, review-ready applications, and interview practice. The most credible distinction is the product behavior behind those claims: source evidence remains visible, missing evidence stays missing, and a person decides what leaves the workspace.

The broad terms “AI resume builder,” “ATS resume checker,” “resume templates,” and “interview prep” have established competitors with clear single-purpose landing pages and free tools. Do not expect a broad all-in-one claim alone to win those intents. Lead with the product's concrete workflow on the home page, make each public tool page satisfy one distinct task, and use a small number of genuinely useful guides to answer questions the tools cannot answer. Treat “truthful/evidence-based AI resume” as a promising positioning and query hypothesis, not an established high-volume keyword category: newer first-party pages use the language, but the observed results do not establish search demand.

Taco is also an ambiguous brand query. Search tests for “Taco” and career software surfaced unrelated acronym and food results alongside no clear, stable independent entity footprint; exact query wording changes what appears. Pair Taco with “career workspace,” “resume,” “job search,” and the TalentConsulting.io company name in public titles, descriptions, headings, and profiles. Do not rename the assistant or infer that people already search for it.

No search-volume, CPC, difficulty, traffic, or ranking-position estimates are included. Search-result observations below are snapshots from this date; results vary by locale, device, personalization, and time.

## Source and confidence conventions

- **Observed:** directly visible in the repository, Taco's public pages, a first-party competitor page, or official Google Search Central guidance linked below.
- **Hypothesis:** an intent or positioning interpretation that needs validation in Search Console, customer interviews, or an experiment.
- **Confidence:** high means the intent is directly apparent in the query and supported by observed pages; medium means adjacent wording or current result patterns support it; low means demand is not established.
- **Priority:** P1 is a near-term core path, P2 is a useful supporting path, and P3 should wait for evidence or capacity.

## Search-result patterns observed

These are qualitative observations across sampled searches on 9 October 2026, not claims about universal or fixed SERP layouts. The samples came from native web search and first-party page checks; their ordering is not treated as Google result order, a stable position, or a reproducible rank measurement.

1. **Task pages dominate high-intent tool searches.** Sampled queries included “ATS resume checker,” “resume matcher,” “AI resume builder,” and “resume templates.” The returned first-party pages show upload/paste steps and match-report language at [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/resume-matcher), a checker at [Teal](https://www.tealhq.com/tool/resume-checker), a builder at [Rezi](https://www.rezi.ai/ai-resume-builder?via=topaitools), and galleries at [Kickresume](https://www.kickresume.com/en/templates/resume/) and [Taco](https://talentconsulting.io/templates). These pages support the task-pattern observation; result ordering does not establish rank.
2. **The same feature is framed differently by different tools.** Jobscan emphasizes match rate, keyword gaps, and a report; Teal combines a checker with its builder and tracker; Huntr leads with job tracking, tailoring, and autofill; Rezi presents an AI resume agent spanning tailoring, job search, and interview preparation. Taco should explain the step that distinguishes it instead of repeating generic “AI-powered” wording.
3. **Templates are a visual-shopping intent.** Competitor galleries label template counts, free versus paid access, ATS posture, and customization/export. Taco's live templates page already says there are 20 options, labels ATS posture, and offers selectable-text PDF plus a linear Word companion. Those specifics are more useful than an unsupported “best ATS resume template” promise.
4. **Interview queries split between interactive practice and static answers.** Taco's live tool page sells role-specific prompts, follow-ups, and proof-focused feedback; the existing role-guide route family covers question-oriented informational searches. Keep those intents separate and link the guide to the practice tool.
5. **Comparison and “best” searches include editorial/list formats.** The sampled query was “best AI resume builders 2026”; the discovery results included Teal's first-party [2026 comparison article](https://www.tealhq.com/post/best-ai-resume-builders). This supports the presence of a comparison format in the sample, not its Google position or a stable SERP pattern. Taco should not publish a self-serving “best tool” roundup without real comparative testing and clear methodology.
6. **Evidence-first positioning is forming, but demand is unproven.** Sampled wording included “evidence-based AI resume builder,” “resume tailoring job description,” and “AI resume tailoring without inventing experience.” First-party pages from [FilterProof](https://filterproof.ai/evidence-based-ai-resume-builder), [Olive Jobs](https://www.olive.jobs/resume-tailoring), and [Applytone](https://applytone.com/) describe evidence-based matching or non-fabricating tailoring. That makes truthful tailoring a meaningful product story and a candidate test cluster. It does not prove that people search this wording often.
7. **Branded discovery is noisy.** Sampled searches included “Taco,” “Taco AI career workspace resume job search,” and “Taco resume builder career software.” The standalone “Taco” result sample included food/dictionary results ([Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/taco), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taco)); the career-qualified sample surfaced Taco's [pricing page](https://talentconsulting.io/pricing) alongside unrelated acronym results such as this [TACO research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02199). This small discovery sample illustrates ambiguity; it does not measure the mix or position of Google results. Pair Taco with “career workspace,” “resume,” “job search,” and TalentConsulting.io in public titles, descriptions, headings, and profiles.

## Current content and architecture assessment

**Observed in repository/public pages:**

- `components/landing/TalentLanding.tsx` leads with “AI job search, grounded in your experience,” explains role-fit gaps and review-ready application drafts, and states that the user submits applications. It links to job search, application preparation, interview practice, privacy, and public tools.
- `components/landing/CareerJourneyDemo.tsx` explains source facts → role fit → review-ready draft. It calls its example illustrative and fictional; preserve that context anywhere it is reused.
- `components/seo/PublicSeoPage.tsx` is a reusable format for public tool/guide pages with a single primary CTA, related tools, content blocks, and a “Source context” link. The format can support stronger task pages but does not by itself ensure distinct, useful content.
- `lib/seo-content.ts` defines role resume examples, industry keyword guides, interview question guides, and `SEO_ROUTES` for those three route families. The examples include precise accomplishments, employers/experience claims, or metrics that are sample copy. They need clear fictional/example labeling and editorial/factual QA; do not present them as Taco customer results, Taco product outcomes, or real candidate facts. Follow the project's rule against fabricated UI or testimonials.
- Public route families include `/tools/*`, `/templates`, `/resume-examples/[role]`, `/resume-keywords/[industry]`, `/interview-questions/[role]`, and `/blog`. The authenticated suite routes (for example `/suite/job-search` and `/suite/resume`) are product destinations, not substitutes for crawlable public task pages.
- The live templates page describes 20 designs, free and Standard groupings, ATS-first/ATS-conscious/Human-first labels, selectable-text PDFs, and linear DOCX companions. The home page says there is one free scout for up to three roles and that prepared packets are review-first.

**Research/implementation hypotheses:**

- The landing-page story already spans multiple user outcomes. Retain it as the company/product overview; avoid retitling it as a narrow ATS checker or resume builder page.
- Prioritize `/tools/ats-analyzer`, `/tools/resume-builder` (including its tailoring workflow), `/tools/interview-prep`, and `/templates`. Keep resume tailoring on the builder today. A separate `/tools/resume-tailoring` page is an optional later experiment only after recorded distinct-query and reader-task evidence, a unique reviewed workflow demo/content, and a context-link plan; otherwise improve the builder and its supporting guide.
- Current SEO route families could become thin or repetitive if expanded automatically. A page should exist only when the role/industry has distinct, reviewed guidance and a useful next action. Prune, consolidate, noindex, or leave unexpanded routes whose content cannot meet that test.
- Keep query ownership explicit: one canonical page per tool job; role/industry guides answer informational queries and point to tools. Avoid multiple `/tools/ats-analyzer`, `/suite/ats-analyzer`, and role-guide pages all targeting “ATS resume checker” without distinct intent.

## Recommended first content pilots

Pilot the first three before extending the existing programmatic guide families. These are content briefs, not drafted product claims.

1. **Resume builder tailoring section** at `/tools/resume-builder`. Target the task “tailor my resume to this job description” within the builder's existing page ownership. Show the actual sequence: use source resume, paste a role description, inspect fit and missing evidence, review controlled edits, choose a template, export or save. Include a clear “not an employer ATS score” statement where matching is discussed, review-before-export, and what happens when evidence is absent. Use a real product capture or clearly labeled fictional illustration; do not invent customer outcomes. Do not create a separate route for this pilot.
2. **ATS checker explainer/tool page refresh** at `/tools/ats-analyzer`. Separate document readability/structure from job-specific requirement matching. Show the output fields the live product actually provides, define any score if present, cite that employers use different systems, and avoid “beat the ATS,” pass guarantees, or unsupported percentages. Let the tool do the work; keep education concise and task-led.
3. **Resume templates chooser** at `/templates`. Preserve the taxonomy and concrete export behavior. Add a clear decision aid about when to select ATS-first, ATS-conscious, or Human-first, what “linear Word companion” means, and a preview that reflects current exports. Link into builder/editor only after describing any account or plan gate accurately.
4. **Interview-practice explainer** at `/tools/interview-prep`. Demonstrate role-specific prompt and follow-up mechanics and explain feedback based on the user's own story/evidence where the current product supports it. Keep informational question guides as supporting resources with genuinely role-specific editorial review.
5. **Evidence-first review guide** (potentially blog or an anchored section on pilot 1). Explain how a job seeker can validate a tailored resume: trace each claim to a source, reject unsupported skills/metrics, preserve unknowns, and review before sending. Ground it in actual product behavior and cite authoritative external advice where relevant. This may better serve users than another role-and-keyword landing page.

For every pilot, require: one clear user task, distinct title/H1, original and accurate substance, visible current product behavior, a suitable CTA, internal links to the next relevant step, a real editorial owner/date, no invented testimonial or measured outcome, and a Search Console annotation after launch.

## Site architecture and cannibalization controls

- **Home `/`:** Taco/TalentConsulting.io overview and cross-workflow value. Use words job seekers recognize (“resume,” “job search,” “application,” “interview”) in descriptive title/H1 and link text while retaining the current human-centered voice.
- **Tools `/tools/*`:** one functional/public page for one task, with a directly usable tool or honest preview. `/tools/ats-analyzer`, `/tools/resume-builder`, and `/tools/interview-prep` should own checker, builder/tailoring, and practice queries respectively. A separate `/tools/resume-tailoring` page is a later optional experiment, not a committed route: require recorded distinct-query and reader-task evidence, unique reviewed workflow demo/content, and a context-link plan first. If that gate is not met, improve the builder section and supporting guide. Do not add a sitemap entry before the gate passes.
- **Templates `/templates`:** browse and compare available formats. Each template detail page should be indexable only if it adds distinctive preview, posture, and export information, not a name swap.
- **Guides `/resume-examples/*`, `/resume-keywords/*`, `/interview-questions/*`, `/blog/*`:** informational intent. Give each route a non-overlapping query family, a purposeful internal link to an appropriate tool, and a consolidation/noindex path when distinct usefulness cannot be maintained.
- **Authenticated suite `/suite/*`:** product/workspace experience, generally private or excluded from indexing according to its auth and robots rules. Do not expose user-generated resumes, applications, job matches, or private career history as public URLs.
- Use breadcrumbs and contextual internal links from relevant guides to their canonical tool. Avoid sitewide exact-match anchors repeated unnaturally.
- Use consistent brand/entity language: “Taco by TalentConsulting.io” or “Taco, the AI career workspace from TalentConsulting.io” at key public touchpoints. Make organization identity and contact/about information discoverable. The company is TalentConsulting.io; Taco is the assistant/product name.

## Phased implementation

### Phase 0 — establish a baseline and protect accuracy

1. Capture current Search Console performance by page/query/country/device, index coverage, sitemap status, and branded-query variants. Export a 90-day baseline and, if available, compare a prior 90-day period; report counts and date ranges, not a guessed market share.
2. Use Google Search Console for aggregate page/query/country/device search data. Use GA4 for outcome measurement only within the authenticated, server-consented cohort; do not report this as a full anonymous organic conversion rate, and do not join search queries to user identities. No new workflow events are recommended here. Consider an event only if a documented decision requires it and the existing consent notice clearly covers that purpose; never log resume or pasted job-description content.
3. Audit every public route's canonical URL, status, robots/indexability, title, H1, description, internal links, and rendered HTML. Specifically verify robots/sitemap because the current working source has `app/robots.ts` deleted/changed; that local dirty-tree observation is not proof of production behavior. Preserve concurrent source changes while doing this audit.
4. Review all `lib/seo-content.ts` facts/example metrics and every page claim against actual product capabilities. Label fiction prominently or replace unsupported prose; no invented benchmarks, endorsements, sample customer outcomes, or employer ATS behavior.

### Phase 1 — strengthen existing high-intent destinations

Refresh the home page metadata/brand descriptors without changing its product promise. Improve the three public task pages (`/tools/ats-analyzer`, `/tools/resume-builder`, `/tools/interview-prep`) and `/templates` with current screenshots, clear task steps, limitations, and CTA/plan disclosures. Keep signup and billing language accurate; do not hardcode plan prices. Link guides to canonical tools.

### Phase 2 — evaluate whether a separate workflow page is justified

Keep tailoring in the `/tools/resume-builder` section while collecting aggregate GSC evidence and reader-task feedback. A separate `/tools/resume-tailoring` route is optional later, only if the evidence shows a distinct query and reader task that the builder page does not satisfy, and the team has a unique reviewed workflow demo/content plus a context-link plan. If that gate fails, improve the builder section and supporting guide. Do not treat competitor wording or sampled query wording as proof of demand, and do not commit the route or add it to the sitemap before the gate passes.

### Phase 3 — maintain selective editorial guides

Audit existing role guides for originality and proof. Retain only pages with accurate, differentiated advice reviewed for the relevant role/industry. Add a small number of guides when customer support, product search logs (privacy-safe), or GSC shows a question that current task pages do not answer. Do not fan out every role × seniority × industry × template permutation.

## Measurement and decision gates

Review monthly after enough data accrues; use a consistent window and annotate deployments. Track:

- Search Console impressions, clicks, CTR, and average position by canonical page and query cluster, split by country/device. Average position is directional and not an individual rank.
- Index coverage, crawl errors, canonical selection, and sitemap discovery for each intended public page.
- Organic entry sessions that complete the intended action: tool run, preview/export, or account-start. Report denominator and attribution window.
- Branded versus nonbranded query mix, including “Taco,” “Taco AI,” “Taco resume,” “TalentConsulting,” and food/acronym confusion where query data is available.
- Page usefulness signals the team already collects lawfully: tool completion, CTA interaction, and short user feedback. Do not equate dwell time with quality or capture resume/JD contents.

Decision rules: improve a page when it receives relevant impressions but weak CTR or weak task completion; test title/description only after verifying search intent and snippet alignment; consolidate or remove pages with no distinct purpose and no evidence of user value; expand a cluster only after a useful page earns relevant impressions or customers repeatedly request the information. Do not declare SEO success based on an isolated rank check.

## Approaches to avoid

- No fabricated customer stories, quotes, before/after numbers, pass rates, interview lifts, “X% more interviews,” or unsupported salary/employer claims.
- No claim that Taco guarantees ATS passage, predicts a hiring decision, or calculates a universal employer ATS score. Describe the product's own measured checks precisely.
- No auto-apply framing. Taco prepares and saves drafts for the user's review; the person controls any external submission.
- No keyword-stuffed role/industry page factory, near-duplicate location pages, or thin query-variant pages. Google's published policies identify doorway abuse, keyword stuffing, and scaled content abuse as spam; AI-assisted drafting does not exempt content from the people-first standard.
- No chasing the naked “Taco” query with unrelated food/acronym pages or adding irrelevant “Taco” repetitions. Use clear, consistent entity descriptors.
- No generic “best resume builder”/“best ATS tool” claims without independently reproducible comparison methodology and clear disclosure. An alternative is a useful, neutral comparison guide that names criteria and limitations.
- No paywalled preview or gated page whose search result promises a free task the visitor cannot actually perform; be explicit about account and tier gates.
- No keyword-volume/CPC/traffic projections based on search result counts, third-party claims, or tool-generated estimates. Use GSC, customer language, and a paid keyword dataset only if the owner later chooses to fund one.

## Final recommendation

Start by strengthening the public task pages and product evidence, with `/tools/resume-builder` owning tailoring queries through a clear workflow section. Preserve the home page as a connected-workflow overview, use guides to answer specific questions, and make “Taco by TalentConsulting.io” explicit wherever the brand first appears. Measure search demand in aggregate and outcomes only in the authenticated, server-consented cohort. Taco's strongest story is a transparent workflow in which job seekers can see what fits, what is missing, what changed, and what they choose to send.
